        Simon says: head (close up)

        It shows a head move, then watches your head for the same one (as in a mirror: its left is your right, unless the mirror-game direction is flipped). Copy it and it moves on; ignore it and it shows you again, gets huffy, then sulks. 3–5 random moves per game. Every copied move also stores a fresh view of your face.

        Simon says: arms (flag signals)

        Its antennas are its arms: laid back = down, horizontal in front = out, straight up = up. It shows one position, you copy it (as in a mirror); then that one and a second, you copy both in order; up to five, like the old Simon toy. Stand back far enough for it to see your shoulders and elbows. If it cannot see your arms it plays the head game instead.

        Dance-along

        When there is a beat (you dancing, music, or the tapped clock with manual groove on) and it can see your arms, its antennas copy your arms live, as in a mirror. Every eight beats it throws in two beats of its own, then copies again.

        What it sees of you

Arms: the angle from hanging down; down under 50°, out, up over 130°. While it is reading your arms it aims a quarter of a frame lower, so your face sits near the top and the rest of you is in shot.

        Kandi trading

        PLUR: peace (arms up at 45°, hands apart) → love (hands together at your chest) → unity (hands clasped low, arms in a V) → respect (one arm up, bent, forearm straight up, fist at head height). It answers each one — down-and-up into a Y for peace, antennas crossed behind its head for love, the Y again for unity — and between them it lays its left ear down and counts your twelve seconds down with the right one. Then it trades: it tilts an ear it is wearing one on until the bracelet slides off the tip into your hand, then holds that same ear out, dead still, for one back. Tell it below which ears are loaded.
        Left and right are the robot's own. A trade button runs the whole exchange from that ear and counts down while it waits; press it again to stop. The ear toggles stay on through a trade, on the assumption you will hang a replacement on it.

        Singing

        Bass songs, built the way the genre is: 140 bpm over a sparse halftime kit (kick on the 1, snare on the 3, offbeat hats) so there is always a backbeat to count against, with a wobbling bass note, wahs, lasers or stabs on top. Fixed four-bar phrases: two bars of kit to find the beat, the A bass, a build whose snare roll stops dead for a quarter-note, the drop on the downbeat of bar 5, B, a fill, C a minor third up, and a last hit left to ring. (The speaker carries nothing under ~300 Hz, so the bass is implied by the harmonics and the moving filter rather than played low; the body bobs at half tempo, on the 1 and the 3.) With singing on it makes up a song every few minutes when it is idle or hanging out with someone, bobs along, and takes a bow. Save the good ones: it mixes saved songs with new ideas. Separately, it hums a made-up little song to itself now and then while pottering about, which no switch turns off: four counted-in taps so you can find the beat, then four (or, now and then, eight) bars of 4/4 on one motif. It sets its own bob to that tempo, so you can groove along with it.
